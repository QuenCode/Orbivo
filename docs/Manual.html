Orbivo – Manual

Player for 360° and 180° videos on the Varjo XR-3. This manual is for the operator at the desktop and for everyone who analyzes the recordings.

1. Overview

Orbivo plays 360° and 180° videos (also 8K and stereo) in the Varjo XR-3. The player computes spatial sound and plays it through normal stereo headphones.

  • Operator: controls everything at the desktop with mouse and keyboard.
  • Participant: wears headset and headphones, sees only the video (or the idle view) and controls nothing. A menu never appears in the headset.

The picture follows only the rotation of the head. If the participant takes a few steps, the picture does not move along: the content stands like a panorama far away.

2. Setting up

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), NVIDIA graphics card (tested: RTX 3070, target: RTX 4080 laptop and RTX 5080) with a current driver.
  • Varjo Base installed and running, the XR-3 connected. Orbivo needs no controllers.
  • Wired stereo headphones without their own surround effects (Windows “Spatial sound” off for this device).
  • Nothing else to install: .NET, FFmpeg, Steam Audio and the OpenXR loader are in the program folder.

Installation and update

  1. Unpack the ZIP Orbivo-<version>-win-x64.zip into a folder the user may write to, e.g. C:\Orbivo or D:\Tools\Orbivo.
  2. Start Orbivo.exe. For everyday use, put a shortcut on the desktop.
  3. Update: close Orbivo, delete or rename the old folder, unpack the new ZIP in the same place. Settings and recordings stay (they are not in the program folder, see Saved settings). Only the diagnostic logs in the folder Logs belong to the program folder; copy them elsewhere first if you want to keep them.

Do not unpack under “C:\Program Files”. Orbivo writes its diagnostic logs into the folder Logs next to the exe. Under “Program Files” it lacks the permission for that without administrator rights.

At the first start Windows SmartScreen may warn (“Windows protected your PC”) because the program is not signed. “More info” → “Run anyway” starts it.

Language

The operator window and this manual are available in English and German. At the very first start Orbivo is English; an installation that already ran an earlier version keeps German. The menu Options → Sprache / Language switches the language. The new language takes effect after a restart, which Orbivo offers right away; the playlist stays. A running video and a running recording end with the restart, so switch the language before the session.

The recordings do not depend on the language: column names, file names and event texts are always German, with a decimal comma (see The recorded data explained).

Start options

Normally Orbivo is started without anything added. For tests without the headset there is --no-xr: a preview window then stands in for the headset, in which you look around with the mouse (drag = turn, mouse wheel = zoom, Home = reset). --lang en or --lang de sets the language for this start only, without changing the saved choice.

Orbivo.exe --no-xr
Orbivo.exe --lang de

3. Course of a session

  1. Start Varjo Base, the headset is connected. Then start Orbivo.
  2. Check the status (on the right): Headset and Session green, Picture output shows about 90 fps, Sound output shows the headphones.
  3. Prepare the recording (if wanted): check the folder, enter “Participant/session” (e.g. P07), switch on “Record”. See Using the recording.
  4. Drag videos into the playlist (the playlist of the last session is already there) and load the first one (double-click). The headset keeps showing the idle view.
  5. The participant puts on headset and headphones and looks in the desired direction. R (Recenter) makes this viewing direction the front of the video.
  6. For a soft start, first F (fade out to black), then Space (Play), then F (fade in).
  7. During playback, watch the big bar above the timeline: it shows what the participant sees right now. The live view shows it as a picture.
  8. At the end: F or B (black), Stop, switch off the recording. The files are in the chosen folder.

Emergency: B makes the headset black at once, M mutes the sound at once. Both always work, also when no video runs, and the same key undoes them.

5. Playlist

  • Add … opens the file dialog. Files and whole folders can also be dragged from Explorer into the window (folders give the videos directly in them, sorted by name, without subfolders). Recognized are .mp4 .m4v .mov .mkv .webm .avi .ts .mts.
  • Load, double-click or Enter loads the selected video. If a video is playing, the new one starts at once; otherwise the headset keeps showing the idle view until Play is pressed. The loaded video is marked with ▶.
  • Remove (Del): the video being shown can only be removed after Stop, so it does not disappear by accident.
  • ▲ ▼ change the order.
  • Playback → Continue with the next video automatically (check mark): at the end of a video the next one starts.
  • Playback → Repeat the current video (loop) (check mark): the current video starts again at its end. Takes precedence over continuing automatically.
  • File → Clear playlist removes all videos after asking. As with removing: not while the loaded video is shown; after Stop it is closed too. The files themselves stay where they are.

The playlist, the last loaded video (it is selected after a restart, but not loaded), continuing automatically, repeating and the fade duration are saved. If a file is missing at the start (e.g. because the external drive is not connected), Orbivo leaves it out and names it in the status under “Playlist”.

6. Playback and keys

The big bar shows what the participant sees right now, e.g. “Headset: Video playing”, “Headset: Idle: Black (video not started yet)” or “Headset: BLACKOUT”. Green means: the participant sees the video picture.

  • Timeline: click or drag to jump.
  • Go to: type a time and press Enter, e.g. 83, 1:23, 1:23.5 or 0:01:23.5.
  • Stop goes back to the start; the headset shows the idle view.
  • Volume acts on Orbivo only, not on the Windows volume.

7. Blackout and fade

  • Blackout (B) makes the headset black and the sound mute at once, whether a video runs or not. The video goes on behind it.
  • Fade (F) fades picture and sound smoothly to black and back. Duration in the field next to it, 0.1 to 30 seconds.
  • Both also act on the idle view. Blackout always stays black, also over the camera picture.
  • The change from the idle view into the video is a hard cut. For a soft transition, fade out first.

8. Recenter

Recenter (R) makes the current viewing direction of the participant the front (center) of the video. Only the turn about the vertical axis counts; the tilt stays as it is. Picture and sound turn together.

  • Also applies to the following videos and after a short loss of the headset connection.
  • Not saved: after a restart of Orbivo the front of the video applies again.
  • Shift+R undoes it. The status line “Recenter” shows the current angle.
  • A recenter in Varjo Base is something else: it moves the reference frame of the headset. Orbivo notes it in the event log.

9. Format of a video

Orbivo recognizes the format from the metadata of the file (Spherical Video, sv3d/st3d) or guesses it from the picture size. The choice “Automatic (…)” shows what was recognized and where it comes from. If something is wrong, change it here.

FieldMeaning
ProjectionEquirect 360°, Equirect 180° (VR180), Cubemap, EAC (YouTube), Fisheye.
Stereo layoutMono, top-bottom (left eye at the top), side-by-side (left eye on the left).
Swap eyesIf the depth looks inside out (near things seem far).
Cube layoutCubemap/EAC only: arrangement of the six faces.
FisheyeField of view, circle center, radius.
OrientationYaw, pitch, roll in degrees, apply with Enter. Positive yaw turns the picture to the left (the former center then lies on the left), positive pitch raises the former center, positive roll tilts counterclockwise.

Every change applies at once and is saved in a small file next to the video, the sidecar (<video>.orbivo.json, e.g. clip.mp4.orbivo.json). It applies again the next time the video is loaded. Whoever copies the video copies the sidecar along. “All automatic” discards the settings of this video.

10. Sound

  • Audio mode (top line, display only): how the sound plays right now, e.g. “Sound fixed to the picture, speakers at ±75°” or “Ambisonics order 1 (AmbiX), binaural, sound field fixed to the picture”. A note after it explains deviations.
  • Sound format: how the channels of the sound track are meant. Usually it stays on Automatic; in brackets is what Orbivo recognized and where from (metadata or guessed from the number of channels).
    • Automatic: Ambisonics according to the metadata in the video. Without metadata, 4, 9 or 16 channels count as Ambisonics AmbiX (order 1, 2 or 3), everything else as channels.
    • Channels: mono and stereo as they are, more channels are mixed down to stereo (e.g. if 4 channels are not Ambisonics).
    • Ambisonics AmbiX: the usual Ambisonics format (channels W Y Z X).
    • Ambisonics FuMa: older recordings (channels W X Y Z), first order only (4 channels).
    If Ambisonics sounds wrong (sound from the wrong side, from above instead of the front, dull), try the other Ambisonics format. On switching, the sound restarts briefly. The choice is saved per video in the sidecar.
  • Ambisonics contains the whole sound field all around. From it Orbivo computes binaurally what the participant hears with the current head position: sounds come from their direction in the video, also from above and below.
  • Sound fixed to the picture (on by default): the sound stays fixed in the video. When the participant turns the head, the sound stays with the picture. With stereo, the left and right channels come from two invisible speakers left and right of the center of the picture; with Ambisonics the whole sound field stays with the picture. Off: the sound turns with the head. Stereo then goes to the headphones unchanged (right for recordings that are binaural already), Ambisonics is always heard as if the participant looked to the front. Saved per video in the sidecar.
  • Width (stereo only): angle of the two speakers to the center of the picture, 30–90°, default 75°.
  • Mono sound has no direction and always stays on both ears.
  • Sound output: the device of the headphones. “Windows default” follows the default device; a device chosen here is never changed automatically. If it is missing, the video plays on silently and Orbivo tries again every 2 seconds. “Refresh” reads the list again, e.g. after plugging in.
  • Options → Lock sound output and volume protects device and volume against accidental changes. The Sound section then says “Locked”. Mute (M) still works. The lock stays after a restart.

Sound is the clock of the playback: the picture follows the sound. The deviation is in the status under “A/V” and should stay within ±20 ms.

11. Headset while idle

What the headset shows while no video runs (before Play, after Stop, without a loaded video). Pause and the end of a video keep showing the video picture.

  • Black (default).
  • See-through (camera picture): the participant sees the room through the cameras of the XR-3. Handy when putting the headset on.
  • Still image: an equirect 360° image (PNG, JPG). Square counts as top-bottom stereo, 4:1 as side-by-side, otherwise mono.

The choice is saved and applies from the start of Orbivo.

12. Live view

“Show” shows about 30 times per second what the participant sees, from their point of view (both eyes combined, with stereo the left half picture), with blackout and fade. It cannot show the camera picture; it then says “See-through”. The live view is off at every start; switched off it costs no computing time.

14. Using the recording

  • Record: every switch-on starts a new pair of files (head CSV and event CSV). Stays on after a restart; a new pair then starts with the start of Orbivo.
  • Only while a video runs: one pair of files per run of a video, the name of the video is in the file name. Play starts it; Stop, the end of the video, unloading, another video or an error end it. Pause stays in the same pair. Nothing is written without a running video. Can only be changed while the recording is off.
  • Folder: default Documents\Orbivo\Aufzeichnungen, saved.
  • Participant/session: optional, becomes part of the file name. Not saved.
  • Head rate: rows per second in the head CSV: every frame (90 Hz), 45, 30 (default) or 10 Hz. Takes effect at once.

Below are the state, the file name and the number of rows. If Orbivo cannot write (folder missing, disk full), the recording ends with a red message; the playback goes on.

The recordings are the same in every language of the window: German column names, German file names, German event texts, decimal comma. This keeps analysis scripts working whichever language the operator uses. The tables below explain every column.

15. The recorded data explained

15.1 Files

Every recording consists of two files with the same beginning (kopf = head, ereignisse = events):

2026-11-08_141203_P07_kopf.csv          head movement, one row per sample
2026-11-08_141203_P07_ereignisse.csv    operation and states, one row per event

with “Only while a video runs”:
2026-11-08_141530_P07_wald_kopf.csv
2026-11-08_141530_P07_wald_ereignisse.csv

Structure: date and time of the start, optionally participant/session, optionally the name of the video (without extension). If the name exists already, Orbivo appends _2, _3 … A file is never overwritten.

Format: separator semicolon, decimal comma, UTF-8 with BOM, first row = column names. A German Excel opens the files correctly with a double-click. In other programs, state when importing: separator ;, decimal separator ,, encoding UTF-8. The files are written continuously (at least every second); even after a crash they are complete up to then.

15.3 Axes and angles

All angles start from the same basic posture: head upright, looking ahead. Orbivo uses the axes of OpenXR: +X to the right, +Y up, −Z ahead (right-handed). The directions of rotation follow the right-hand rule about each axis. This gives:

Order: read the three angles like this: first turn by yaw about the vertical axis of the room, then tilt the head by pitch about its own transverse axis, then by roll about its own viewing axis. Yaw and pitch therefore describe the viewing direction (like longitude and latitude), roll only the tilt about this direction. If the participant looks almost straight up or down, yaw is ambiguous; Orbivo then sets it to 0 and counts the turn in roll.

Reference of Kopf_…: the room as Varjo Base defines it (“ahead” is the direction that Varjo Base set as ahead during setup or at the last recenter in Varjo Base). Recenter and the orientation of the video in Orbivo do not change these columns.

15.4 Head and view in the video

For most analyses Blick_Video_… is the right column: it says which spot of the video the participant looks at. It takes recenter and the orientation of the video (Format → Orientation) into account. Kopf_…, on the other hand, is the bare head posture in the room.

Without pitch and roll in the orientation of the video (both 0 there):

Blick_Video_Yaw   = Kopf_Yaw − Recenter_Yaw − orientation yaw     (brought to −180 … 180)
Blick_Video_Pitch = Kopf_Pitch

Without recenter and without orientation both pairs of columns are equal. The orientation of a video is in the event log (event “Format”) and in the sidecar. The view has no roll column: for the question “where does the participant look”, roll does not matter.

Important: “view” here means the head direction, i.e. the center of the field of view of the headset. Orbivo does not measure the eye movement within the field of view (no eye tracking). The field of view of the XR-3 is about 115° wide, so the participant can also see things that lie up to a good 50° beside the viewing direction.

Example

Sekunden ;Videozeit_s;Kopf_Yaw_Grad;Kopf_Pitch_Grad;Blick_Video_Yaw_Grad;Blick_Video_Pitch_Grad;Recenter_Yaw_Grad
12,033333;12,000     ;50,00        ;-10,00         ;30,00               ;-10,00                ;20,00

At the recenter the participant looked 20° to the left; that became the center of the picture. Now their head is turned 50° to the left and 10° down. In the video they therefore look 30° left of the center of the picture, 10° below the horizon, at the frame at second 12.0 of the video.

15.5 From the angle to the pixel

With equirect videos the view converts directly into a spot in the video picture. W and H are the width and height of one eye in pixels (top-bottom: half the height, top picture = left eye; side-by-side: half the width, left picture = left eye; mono: the whole picture). Yaw and pitch are Blick_Video_Yaw_Grad and Blick_Video_Pitch_Grad.

Projectionx (from the left)y (from the top)
Equirect 360°x = W · (0.5 − yaw / 360)y = H · (0.5 − pitch / 180)
Equirect 180° (VR180)x = W · (0.5 − yaw / 180), only for −90 ≤ yaw ≤ 90y = H · (0.5 − pitch / 180)

Example: an 8K top-bottom video has 8192 × 8192 pixels, one eye therefore 8192 × 4096. For yaw 30°, pitch −10° this gives x = 8192 · (0.5 − 30/360) ≈ 3413 and y = 4096 · (0.5 + 10/180) ≈ 2276 (in the top half picture).

For cubemap, EAC and fisheye there is no such simple formula; there, read yaw and pitch as a direction in space.

15.6 Quaternion

The four columns Kopf_qw, Kopf_qx, Kopf_qy, Kopf_qz describe the same head rotation as yaw/pitch/roll, but without ambiguity and without a jump at ±180°. They are meant for exact analyses (e.g. in Python, R or MATLAB).

  • Unit quaternion (length 1), order w, x, y, z; w = 1, x = y = z = 0 means: head upright, looking ahead.
  • It rotates directions from the head into the room, in the axes named above (+X right, +Y up, −Z ahead). This is the convention of OpenXR, which Varjo Base delivers.
  • q and −q mean the same rotation; a change of sign of all four values is therefore not a jump.

The viewing direction as a vector (length 1) and from it yaw and pitch:

ahead_x = −2·(qx·qz + qw·qy)
ahead_y = −2·(qy·qz − qw·qx)
ahead_z = −(1 − 2·(qx² + qy²))

yaw   = atan2(−ahead_x, −ahead_z)
pitch = asin(ahead_y)

15.7 Time and rate

  • Sekunden starts at 0 at the start of the pair of files. With “Only while a video runs”, 0 is at the Play command that started the video; the first video frame comes shortly after.
  • In the headset, the time of every head row is the moment the frame was shown in the headset (the predicted display time that Varjo Base delivers for every frame, shortly after rendering). Head posture, video time and brightness of a row therefore match what the participant saw at that moment.
  • Rate: the headset makes 90 frames per second. At 45, 30 or 10 Hz Orbivo takes every 2nd, 3rd or 9th of them. The intervals are therefore multiples of 11.1 ms and vary slightly. For analyses use the column Sekunden, not the row number.
  • If the headset fails or is disconnected, no rows come; there is then a gap in Sekunden. The event log names the disconnection and the reconnection.
  • The time zone is in the first event. When the clocks change, Uhrzeit jumps, Sekunden does not.

15.9 Tips for the analysis

  • Discard rows with Tracking = 0; for analyses of “what did they see in the video” take only rows with Inhalt = Video, Helligkeit > 0 and Status = Läuft (or Pause).
  • Yaw jumps from +180 to −180 behind. For means and speeds “unwrap” the angle (e.g. numpy.unwrap) or compute with vectors or quaternions.
  • Angle between the view and a target in the video (target at yaw yT, pitch pT), in an English Excel, with yaw in column P and pitch in column Q: 
    =DEGREES(ACOS(SIN(RADIANS(Q2))*SIN(RADIANS(pT)) + COS(RADIANS(Q2))*COS(RADIANS(pT))*COS(RADIANS(P2-yT))))
    Replace yT and pT by the angles of the target or by cell references. Result in degrees, 0 = looking straight at the target. An English Excel may need the import settings above (separator ;, decimal separator ,).
  • With Python (pandas): 
    import pandas as pd
head = pd.read_csv("2026-11-08_141203_P07_kopf.csv", sep=";", decimal=",", encoding="utf-8-sig")
video = head[(head.Tracking == 1) & (head.Inhalt == "Video") & (head.Helligkeit > 0)]
print(video[["Videozeit_s", "Blick_Video_Yaw_Grad", "Blick_Video_Pitch_Grad"]].describe())
  • To compare with the video: Videozeit_s is the time in the video, as a video player shows it too.

The video stuttersStatus “Decoder”: software decoding or “The buffer runs empty” means the video is too heavy for this computer (e.g. 8K at 60 fps). Close other programs, plug in the power supply (laptop).
The recording stops with a red messageFolder reachable? Space on the disk? Choose the folder again and switch it on again.
FFmpeg / Steam Audio / OpenXR redProgram files incomplete: unpack the ZIP again.
The window is in the wrong languageChoose the language in the menu Options → “Sprache / Language” and let Orbivo restart.
